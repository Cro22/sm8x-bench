# H0 results — MAX kernels on consumer NVIDIA GPUs

**Status: partial.** All MAX kernels (GEMV/matmul fp16/bf16, Q4_0 via its real
dispatcher, attention decode), the measured roofline, the **llama.cpp Q4_0/Q8_0/
Q4_K GEMV baselines**, the **cuBLAS fp16 GEMV** dense ceiling, and **our Q4_0
kernel**, and the **FlashInfer decode-attention baseline** are done on the RTX
3090, all under a per-run-verified 1695 MHz clock. The one remaining baseline
(llama.cpp flash-attn) is pending. This report was corrected after an adversarial
review (see "Corrections" below).

## Result

On the RTX 3090 (sm_86), MAX's dense decode GEMV is at the memory-bandwidth limit
(fp16/bf16 M=1 at **90–97 % of spec** across all projection shapes) and its
attention decode is at the limit at long context (**91.0 % of spec at 16k**, a hair
ahead of FlashInfer) with a narrow **mid-context gap**: at seq 4096 MAX is 63.7 %
vs FlashInfer's **70.2 % on the same paged KV** (~6–7 points, same-session 3 passes
each, non-overlapping distributions; see caveats). Its
**Q4_0 (4-bit)** path, measured through its real dispatcher, is **uneven**: a
decode-tuned config gives **69–81 %** on up_proj/down_proj, but shapes that fall
to the default 128×128 GEMM tile (gate_up/lm_head) run at **~15 %**, and
group_size-32 (GGUF Q4_0) **fails to compile** on o_proj/qkv. **llama.cpp** is a
strong Q4_0 baseline everywhere (**73–100 %** of spec). **Our kernel**
(`kernels/q4_0_gemv.mojo`), after tuning (multiple output rows per warp for
memory-level parallelism + per-shape launch config), reaches **74–102 %** of spec
on all six shapes and is at **parity with llama.cpp Q4_0** — *not* faster. Measured
**same-session, 3 passes each, all preserved** (least-contended min-pass, since a
locked graphics clock does not lock the GDDR6X memory clock and the shared desktop
steals bandwidth → 0–9 % run-to-run spread): five of six shapes are **ties within
that noise** (o_proj 74.5 vs 72.7, qkv 82.5 vs 83.8, up_proj 88.1 vs 88.4,
down_proj 92.0 vs 94.7, lm_head 101.9 vs 101.5), and llama.cpp is **clearly faster
on gate_up (100.1 vs 91.8)**. No shape shows a robust win for ours. (The earlier
"beats llama.cpp on four of six" was an artifact of comparing a quiet-GPU *ours*
run against a different-day *llama* run; fair same-session measurement erases every
claimed win — see Corrections.) **The real, robust result is versus MAX**: on the
**four shapes MAX can run**, ours is faster — ~6× on gate_up/lm_head (MAX falls to
the default GEMM, ~15 %), and ahead on up_proj (88 vs 68) and down_proj (92 vs 81);
on the other **two (o_proj/qkv) MAX compile-fails, so there is no MAX speed to
beat** — ours simply runs them. The contribution is a *uniform* decode-quant kernel
that is faster than MAX everywhere MAX produces a number, runs where MAX can't, and
**matches** the best CUDA baseline on consumer Ampere.

## Setup

- GPU: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 (sm_86, GA102, 82 SMs), driver 610.62, CUDA
  driver 13.3. Power limit 420 W (raised). **No RTX 4090 (sm_89) on this box** —
  sm_89 rows are N/A until a 4090 is available.
- Clocks: graphics clock locked to **1695 MHz** for every measurement, verified
  holding under sustained load. Memory clock not lockable on GeForce (sits at max,
  ~9751 MHz). Locking is done from the Windows host (`nvidia-smi -lgc 1695,1695`);
  WSL cannot lock. See `scripts/gpu-lock.sh` and the `bench-methodology` skill.
- Toolchain: Mojo 1.0.0 (ed45d567), max 26.5.0 (PyPI). `modular` submodule pinned
  to tag `max/v26.5.0`, SHA `b4497b7ce9ba96331c72c637ad41b44bab374f33`.
- Timing: **nsys per-kernel duration** (`--trace=cuda`), the authoritative number
  (excludes host-side dispatch overhead, which is amortized in production, and is
  robust to the shared-desktop GPU). Correctness gated on relative L2 error
  `||got − ref||₂ / ||ref||₂`. Dates: 2026-09-01 (MAX, llama.cpp, attention);
  2026-09-02 (tuned "ours" Q4_0 re-measurement).
- Env, GPU state, and SHAs are captured per result in `bench/results/*.json` via
  `bench/env.py`; no number here is hand-typed (`bench/report.py` generates the
  tables).

## Reading the results

- **Dense GEMV (fp16/bf16), M=1: at the bandwidth limit.** 87–98 % of spec,
  rising toward peak as N grows (lm_head, N=128256, is highest at ~98 %). fp16
  and bf16 are at parity — both take the same `gemv_split_k_float16/bfloat16`
  kernel (confirmed by nsys). This is a clean "upstream is already good here"
  result; there is no GEMV gap to close on consumer Ampere.
- **Dense M=8: 68–89 % of spec.** Lower than M=1 because M=8 moves 8× the
  activations for the same weights; still memory-class. fp16 M>1 is served by
  cuBLAS (`ampere_fp16_s16816gemm`), bf16 M>1 by MAX's own `multistage_gemm`;
  both measured, at parity here.
- **cuBLAS fp16 GEMV (dense ceiling) is NOT the M=1 ceiling on consumer Ampere —
  MAX beats it.** cuBLAS dispatches a **tensor-core GEMM tile**
  (`ampere_s16816gemm` / a cutlass wmma kernel) for five of six shapes at M=1 —
  wasting most of a 64-row tile on a single row — and a real GEMV (`gemv2T_kernel`)
  only for lm_head (N=128256). Result: cuBLAS is **79–93 %** of spec on the
  GEMM-tile shapes and 105 % on lm_head, while MAX's dedicated `gemv_split_k_fp16`
  is **90–98 %** on *all* shapes. Per shape MAX vs cuBLAS: o_proj 90.6 vs **79.2**,
  qkv 93.5 vs 88.8, up_proj 96.0 vs 84.4, down_proj 93.7 vs 92.8, gate_up 96.9 vs
  90.0, lm_head 97.6 vs **105.1** (only here cuBLAS's true GEMV edges ahead). So the
  usual "cuBLAS is the vendor ceiling" holds only where cuBLAS actually runs a
  GEMV; for decode-shaped fp16 GEMV MAX's split-K kernel is the better dense
  choice. (fp16, CUBLAS_COMPUTE_32F / fp32 accum, L2 ~3e-6 — validated.)
- **Q4_0 (4-bit), MAX real dispatch: uneven, not a flat "gap".** Measured through
  the public `matmul_gpu_qint4[g32]` with static shapes (the real per-shape
  config, not a forced fallback): up_proj **68 %** and down_proj **81 %** get a
  decode-tuned BM=16/BK=32 config and are competitive; gate_up and lm_head fall to
  the default 128×128 GEMM tile and run at **~15 %** (at M=1 that tile uses 1/128
  of its rows); o_proj/qkv **compile-fail** for g32 (BK=128 config, see caveats).
  So MAX is not uniformly slow — it lacks a single decode-quant kernel that works
  well on every shape.
- **Q4_0 three-way (M=1, nsys per-kernel, same weights): ours ≈ llama.cpp (parity),
  both ≫ MAX.** llama.cpp's `mul_mat_vec_q` (+`quantize_q8_1`, graphs off) is
  **73–100 %** of spec; ours (`dp4a`/Q8_1, rows-per-warp + per-shape tuning) is
  **74–102 %**. Measured **same-session, 3 passes each, all preserved**; on the
  least-contended min-pass, five of six are ties within the 0–9 % run-to-run band
  (o_proj +1.8, qkv −1.3, up_proj −0.4, down_proj −2.7, lm_head +0.4) and
  **llama.cpp is clearly faster on gate_up (100.1 vs 91.8)** — **no shape is a
  robust ours win.** The earlier "beats 4/6" compared a quiet-GPU ours run to a
  different-day llama run and is retracted (see Corrections). Versus MAX real
  dispatch ours IS faster on all six: ~6× on gate_up/lm_head (MAX default GEMM),
  ahead on up_proj (88 vs 68) and down_proj (92 vs 81), and it runs o_proj/qkv
  where MAX compile-fails. See the H1 section.
- **llama.cpp Q8_0 and Q4_K GEMV: bandwidth-saturated, no MAX GPU counterpart.**
  Same `mul_mat_vec_q` (+`quantize_q8_1`) family, nsys-verified (kernel template
  arg 8 = Q8_0, 12 = Q4_K). **Q8_0** (1.0625 B/weight): 80–103 % of spec,
  ≥99 % on the four mid/large shapes — it is DRAM-bound and llama.cpp reaches it.
  **Q4_K** (0.5625 B/weight, K-quant superblocks): 74–104 %, same shape trend as
  Q4_0. MAX has **no GPU matmul for either** (Q8_0/Q4_K are CPU-only upstream, see
  audit) — so these rows are a llama.cpp-only baseline; the `max` column is N/A by
  absence, not a measured failure. The Q4_K weights are quantized with ggml's own
  quantizer (`quantize_weight`, since gguf-py implements dequant but not quant for
  K-quants) and validated against the Python dequant (L2 ~5e-3). A few large-N
  rows read slightly **above the 936 GB/s nominal spec** (up to ~104 %): the shape
  is pure weight streaming and the GDDR6X sustains marginally over the nominal
  figure — `% spec` is normalized to that nominal, not capped at it.
- **Attention decode: a narrow MAX mid-context gap — confound removed, measured
  same-session.** MAX `mha_decoding` (+`mha_splitk_reduce`) and FlashInfer's **paged**
  path (`BatchDecodeWithPagedKVCacheWrapper`, page 128 = MAX's layout) measured
  **same-session, 3 passes each** (medians; distributions below):

  | seq | MAX (3×) | FlashInfer paged (3×) | FlashInfer contiguous¹ |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1024 | 25.6 (25.6–25.6) | 39.2 (39.1–39.3) | 27.0 |
  | 4096 | **63.7 (60.0–63.8)** | **70.2 (69.7–73.7)** | 79.3 |
  | 16384 | **91.0 (90.9–91.0)** | 89.9 (89.5–90.0) | 89.7 |

  ¹ contiguous FlashInfer (`single_decode_with_kv_cache`) is from an earlier
  session — context only; the paged column is the apples-to-apples one.

  Reading (MAX validated vs its own `mha_gpu_naive`, L2 ~5e-4; both nsys per-kernel,
  clock 1695): **at long context MAX is at the roofline and a hair *ahead* of
  FlashInfer** (91.0 vs 89.9 — the earlier "MAX 86.7" was a slow single run). At
  **seq 4096 there is a real ~6–7-point gap** (MAX 63.7 vs 70.2) — the two 3-pass
  distributions do not overlap (MAX max 63.8 < FlashInfer min 69.7), so it survives
  the run-to-run noise. At seq 1024 both are latency-bound (tiny absolute; FlashInfer's
  paged kernel has less short-seq overhead). Net: MAX decode attention is
  competitive-to-best at long context; the only real deficit is a **narrow
  mid-context (seq~4k) gap**. Cause **inferred** (split-K partitioning / occupancy at
  seq 4096 on 82 SMs), not ncu-measured → a tuning question, not a bug. Much of the
  original "+17.6-point" gap was the paged-vs-contiguous confound (contiguous
  FlashInfer read 79.3 %; paging it costs ~9 pts). FlashInfer 0.6.18 / torch
  2.14+cu130 in `.venv-attn`.

## Anomalies and caveats

- **Q4_0 compile-fail for group_size 32 (o_proj/qkv), real — reproduced through
  the public dispatcher.** Their tuned m≤32 config uses BK=128, so
  `group_size // BK = 32 // 128 = 0` → an `int_tuple` out-of-bounds at
  `qmatmul_gpu.mojo:873` (full compiler output committed as the
  `results/*.compile_error.txt` sidecars). It is a MAX limitation, not a harness
  setup error: the *identical* harness at the same `group_size=32` compiles and
  runs validated for up_proj/down_proj (BK=32 configs) and fails only on
  o_proj/qkv. The table shows these as `compile-fail`. See
  `reports/open-questions.md`.
- **The earlier "down_proj crashes" claim is RETRACTED.** That
  CUDA_ERROR_ILLEGAL_ADDRESS came from a first harness that *forced* the BM=128
  default config; MAX's real dispatch for down_proj uses BM=16 and runs cleanly
  (43.6 µs / 81 %). The unmasked-A-load OOB is a genuine *latent* kernel bug
  (`reports/max-q4_0-analysis.md`) but is not reached by the shipping dispatcher
  at these shapes.
- **`% meas` exceeds 100 % on the fastest kernels.** The bandwidth probe
  (`bw_probe`, ~791–816 GB/s) under-measures true achievable bandwidth; the best
  GEMVs reach 870–900 GB/s. The probe (a read-only reduction) needs more
  in-flight loads to be a true ceiling. `% spec` is the reliable column.
- **Provenance & what is / isn't preserved (hardened after a second review).**
  Each GEMV JSON records `gpu.graphics_clock_mhz_observed` sampled under load (all
  1695), the **sha256 + byte count of every input** it read (`inputs`), and — for
  the same-session ours/llama Q4_0 re-measurement — **all 3 pass medians and the
  per-kernel nsys distribution** (`timing.passes`, `timing.pass_detail`,
  per-kernel min/med/max/stddev). So those numbers are auditable from the JSON, not
  just a collapsed median. **Not yet preserved:** the raw `.nsys-rep`/CSV files
  (only the parsed gpukernsum stats are kept), and the multi-GB input `.bin`s are
  gitignored (their hashes are recorded, so identity is checkable, but the bytes
  are not versioned). The **bandwidth-probe** JSON predates observed-clock sampling
  and records only the CLI-passed clock — the "observed per run" guarantee covers
  the GEMV/attention runs, not the probe.
- **The locked graphics clock does NOT lock the memory clock**, and these kernels
  are memory-bound — so for bandwidth the relevant clock (GDDR6X, not lockable on
  GeForce) is uncontrolled, and the Windows desktop compositing on the same GPU
  steals bandwidth. Net run-to-run spread is **0–9 %** (not the ±0.3 % a single
  quiet burst suggests). Per-shape differences smaller than that band are **not**
  claimed; the ours-vs-llama Q4_0 comparison is same-session and read as parity.
- **Not every result got the 3-pass treatment.** The ours/llama **Q4_0** headline
  is 3-pass same-session with all pass medians + input hashes in the JSON. The
  **attention** MAX-vs-FlashInfer(paged) comparison is 3-pass same-session too (the
  per-seq ranges are shown in the attention table), but only the median run's JSON
  is retained — the attention runners don't yet store `passes[]`. The remaining
  baselines (MAX GEMV, Q8_0/Q4_K, cuBLAS, FlashInfer-contiguous) are **single
  representative nsys runs** — read their point estimates with the same 0–9 %
  caution, and treat sub-5 % differences as ties.
- **`harness_sha` is `…-dirty` on most JSONs (53 of 68).** The base commit is
  recorded and `bench/env.py` ignores generated outputs when computing dirtiness,
  but a JSON does **not** capture the exact uncommitted source diff that produced
  it — measurements were taken from a working tree mid-edit. Reproduce from the
  committed code at the recorded SHA; the diff is not preserved per result.
- **Roofline byte count is ~0.1–0.2 % low for the quant GEMVs.**
  `roofline.gemv_bytes` counts the activation and output as 2 B each, but llama.cpp
  consumes **F32** activations and both llama.cpp and ours emit **F32** output
  (4 B). Since the weight term dominates at M=1 (e.g. o_proj weights 9.4 MB vs x+y
  ~24 KB), the undercount is <0.2 % of total bytes → achieved GB/s and `% spec` are
  ~0.2 % *conservative*, changing no conclusion. (The dense fp16 rows use 2 B
  correctly.)
- **The README summary table is a hand-transcribed rounded copy** of these
  JSON-generated tables (only the tables in *this* file are `bench/report.py`
  output); the numbers agree but the README table is not itself generated.
- **fp16 M>1 accumulates in fp16** (cuBLAS default); flagged in each JSON's
  tolerance field. Element-wise error is large on cancellation-heavy outputs,
  which is why correctness is gated on L2, not per-element rel error.

## H1 result — our Q4_0 decode GEMV (impl "ours")

The dense and attention paths need nothing. For Q4_0 we wrote a decode GEMV
(`kernels/q4_0_gemv.mojo`) that is uniformly near-roofline on all six shapes and
at **parity** with llama.cpp. M=1, nsys per-kernel, identical Q4_0 weight bytes,
L2 ~5e-3, **ours and llama.cpp measured same-session, 3 passes each, all preserved
in `timing.passes`** (median-of-3 shown; the min-pass = least-contended is the
fairer number since a locked graphics clock does NOT lock the GDDR6X memory clock
and the shared desktop steals bandwidth — run-to-run spread 0–9 %):

| shape (N×K) | MAX (real dispatch) | **ours** (med / min%) | llama.cpp (med / min%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| o_proj 4096×4096 | compile-fail (g32) | **13.7 µs / 73.8 / 74.5** | 13.9 µs / 72.6 / 72.7 |
| qkv 6144×4096 | compile-fail (g32) | **19.6 µs / 77.4 / 82.5** | 18.1 µs / 83.7 / 83.8 |
| down_proj 4096×14336 | 43.5 µs / 81.2 % | **38.4 µs / 92.0 / 92.0** | 38.0 µs / 92.9 / 94.7 |
| up_proj 14336×4096 | 51.6 µs / 68.4 % | **41.7 µs / 84.8 / 88.1** | 39.9 µs / 88.5 / 88.5 |
| gate_up 28672×4096 | 474 µs / 14.9 % | **77.0 µs / 91.7 / 91.8** | 70.6 µs / 100.1 / 100.1 |
| lm_head 128256×4096 | 2031 µs / 15.6 % | **312 µs / 101.2 / 101.9** | 311 µs / 101.5 / 101.5 |

Honest reading: **ours is at parity with llama.cpp — not faster.** On the fairer
least-contended (min-pass) numbers, five of six shapes are ties within the 0–9 %
noise band (o_proj +1.8, qkv −1.3, up_proj −0.4, down_proj −2.7, lm_head +0.4) and
**llama.cpp is clearly faster on gate_up (100.1 vs 91.8)**. No shape is a robust
win for ours. **The robust result is versus MAX**, where ours is faster on all
six: ~6× on gate_up/lm_head (MAX default GEMM, 15 %), ahead on up_proj (88 vs 68)
and down_proj (92 vs 81), and it runs the two shapes MAX compile-fails. It is a
*single* kernel, uniformly 74–102 % of roofline, where MAX is either absent
(compile-fail), 15 %, or (at best) 81 %. The value is uniform coverage at the best
CUDA baseline's level, not beating it.

Mechanism: two levers stacked. (1) The naive fp32 dequant path plateaued at ~41 %
even after wide loads, ILP, and smem staging — **diagnosed by elimination as
compute-bound on the per-weight int→float converts** (NOT proven with Nsight
Compute: `ncu` is blocked on this box, ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM, so this is an inferred
diagnosis, not a measured roofline). The fix is llama.cpp's — quantize the
activations to Q8_1 (int8 + per-block scale) once, then do the
Q4_0·Q8_1 dot with `dp4a.s32.s32` (int8 DP4A, inline PTX — no stdlib dp4a on
sm_86), unpacking nibbles with a `0x0F0F0F0F` mask. That alone plateaued at ~62–91 %
(one load chain per warp). (2) **Multiple output rows per warp** (`ROWS_PER_WARP`)
then reuses the shared x-quants across rows and gives each warp that many
independent weight-load chains — the memory-level parallelism that lifts every
shape to roofline. Tuned per shape (`ROWS_PER_WARP`/`ROWS_PER_BLOCK`): small/mid N
want 4 rows/warp; the two near-roofline giants want 2/2. Two dead ends measured
and discarded: a split (dual) accumulator (neutral → confirms memory-bound, not
reduction-latency-bound), and folding the quantize pass into the GEMV
(~2× slower — a register-serial per-lane quantize doesn't hide). Remaining: M>1,
and the residual quantize-launch overhead on the smallest shapes.

## Corrections (adversarial review)

The first pass of this report claimed "MAX 6–15 % / ours 6–10× faster / MAX
crashes on down_proj." A review showed the MAX column had been measured through a
*forced* `multistage_gemm_q` config (128×128×32) rather than MAX's real
dispatcher, that clocks were not proven per run, and that llama.cpp was timed
with CUDA graphs on (1 kernel instance). All three are fixed here: MAX is
measured via `matmul_gpu_qint4[g32]` (real per-shape config), the SM clock is
sampled under load and recorded per JSON (1695), and llama.cpp runs with graphs
disabled (131 instances). The corrected picture is above.

**Second review (methodology / overclaiming).** A later review found the report
still oversold rigor. Fixed here: (1) **"beats llama.cpp on four of six" is
retracted** — it compared a quiet-GPU *ours* run against a *different-day* llama
run; re-measured **same-session, 3 passes each**, every claimed win disappears and
ours ↔ llama.cpp is **parity** (llama.cpp is faster on gate_up). (2) **"medians of
3 passes" is now literally true and preserved** — the runner takes 3 nsys passes
and stores all pass medians + the per-kernel distribution in `timing.passes`; the
old wording described a single run. (3) **Input provenance**: the ours/llama Q4_0
results now record the sha256 of the exact W/x/ref bytes they read. (4) **"compute-bound" is
relabeled an inferred diagnosis** (ncu is blocked on this box, so it is not a
measured roofline). (5) The **memory clock is uncontrolled** and the shared desktop
contends — the honest run-to-run band is 0–9 %, not ±0.3 %. (6) The **attention
"nothing to improve"** line contradicted the seq-4096 gap and is fixed. (7) The
**bandwidth-probe clock** is CLI-only (not observed) and the **README summary table
is a manual copy** — both now stated. Remaining gap: raw `.nsys-rep`/CSV artifacts
are still not versioned.

**Third review (approved as experimental benchmark; residual fixes).** Fixed here:
(1) stale README attention text (still 79.3 %/confounded/pending) updated to the
resolved result. (2) The seq-4096 attention gap is no longer stated on single runs:
**re-measured MAX and paged FlashInfer same-session, 3 passes each** — MAX 63.7 %
(60.0–63.8) vs FlashInfer 70.2 % (69.7–73.7), non-overlapping, so the ~6–7-pt gap
survives noise; and at seq 16384 MAX (91.0) is actually a hair ahead (the earlier
"86.7 / MAX behind" was a slow single run). The cause stays an *inferred* tuning
gap, not asserted. (3) The input-hash claim is scoped to ours/llama Q4_0 (the MAX
JSONs have none). (4) The `-dirty` `harness_sha` (53/68) and (7) the ~0.2 %
byte-count undercount for F32-activation/output quant rows are now declared as
limitations. (5) "faster than MAX on all six" → faster on the four MAX can run;
runs the two it compile-fails (no MAX speed to beat there). (6) fixed the up_proj
llama min-pass rounding (88.4→88.5; both med and min are 88.45 %).
